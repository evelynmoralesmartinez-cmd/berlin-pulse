# Un lugar por distrito. Un distrito por párrafo. Verificado por Eve.

Berlin has 12 districts (Bezirke). Each district has its own character and places of interest.

Mitte district, Berlin – Reichstag: The Reichstag is the building of the German parliament. It was built in the neo-Renaissance style and opened in 1894.

Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg district, Berlin – East Side Gallery: The East Side Gallery is the longest remaining section of the Berlin Wall, about 1.3 kilometres long. The Wall separated East Berlin (GDR) from Kreuzberg, in the American sector of West Berlin. In 1990, artists from all over the world were invited to paint messages about joy, relief and hope after the end of the Cold War and the reunification of Germany. The murals of the 118 artists who took part can still be seen today, which makes it an open-air art gallery.

Pankow district, Berlin – Mauerpark: Mauerpark is a public park in Prenzlauer Berg. Its name means "Wall Park", because it lies on a former section of the Berlin Wall and its death strip. The park is on the border between Prenzlauer Berg, in former East Berlin, and Gesundbrunnen, in former West Berlin.

Charlottenburg-Wilmersdorf district, Berlin – Charlottenburg Palace: Charlottenburg Palace is a baroque palace and the largest palace in Berlin. It was built at the end of the 17th century for Sophie Charlotte, the wife of Elector Frederick III of Brandenburg, who later became King Frederick I in Prussia. The New Wing was added from 1740 for Frederick II (Frederick the Great).

Spandau district, Berlin – Spandau Citadel: The Spandau Citadel is one of the best-preserved Renaissance military fortresses in Europe. It was built in the 16th century and is surrounded by water. Today it houses museums and historical exhibitions, and it is a popular venue for concerts and cultural festivals.

Steglitz-Zehlendorf district, Berlin – Botanical Garden: The Botanical Garden of Berlin has more than 20,000 plant species and is one of the most important botanical gardens in the world. Its highlight is the Great Tropical Greenhouse (Großes Tropenhaus), a masterpiece of iron-and-glass architecture that recreates a tropical ecosystem. Website: bo.berlin/en

Tempelhof-Schöneberg district, Berlin – Tempelhofer Feld: Tempelhofer Feld is the former Tempelhof Airport, famous for the Berlin Airlift during the Cold War. Its huge terminal building was built during the Nazi era. Today it is a giant public park with its original runways, where Berliners go skating, walking, land sailing or having barbecues.

Neukölln district, Berlin – Britzer Garten: Britzer Garten is a large park designed for the Federal Garden Show of 1985. It offers a peaceful natural escape from the busy city, with lakes, rose gardens, a large traditional windmill and its famous tulip and dahlia blooming seasons.

Treptow-Köpenick district, Berlin – Köpenick Palace: Köpenick Palace is a baroque palace on a small island in the river Dahme. It houses a section of the Museum of Decorative Arts (Kunstgewerbemuseum). The old town of Köpenick around it is one of the most picturesque lake areas in Berlin.

Marzahn-Hellersdorf district, Berlin – Gardens of the World (Gärten der Welt): The Gardens of the World are a large landscape park with traditional gardens from different cultures, including one of the largest Chinese gardens in Europe, and Japanese, Balinese, Renaissance and Oriental gardens. The park also has a modern cable car.

Lichtenberg district, Berlin – Stasi Museum: The Stasi Museum is located in the original headquarters of the Ministry for State Security of the GDR. The offices of the top communist officials are preserved as they were. It is a powerful historical visit to understand the system of spying and control during the division of Germany. Website: stasimuseum.de

Reinickendorf district, Berlin – Tegel Palace (Schloss Tegel or Humboldt Palace): Tegel Palace, also called Humboldt Palace, is a small country house in Tegel, in the Reinickendorf district of Berlin. The brothers Wilhelm and Alexander von Humboldt spent much of their childhood in an earlier palace on this site and on the estate, which extends almost as far as Lake Tegel.

Reinickendorf district, Berlin – how to visit Tegel Palace: Tegel Palace can only be visited on guided tours. The tours are in German and usually take place on Mondays. Around the palace there are paths and trails where you can walk to Lake Tegel.
