# Typical food and sweets in Berlin. One dish per paragraph. Verified by Eve.

Typical food in Berlin – Currywurst: Currywurst is a sliced pork sausage covered in curry ketchup and curry powder, usually served with fries or a bread roll. It was invented in Berlin in 1949 by Herta Heuwer. Famous places to try it are Curry 36 in Kreuzberg and Konnopke's Imbiss under the U-Bahn tracks in Prenzlauer Berg.

Typical food in Berlin – Döner Kebab: The döner kebab in bread, with meat, salad and sauce, became popular in Berlin in the 1970s thanks to Turkish immigrants. Berlin is often called the home of the modern döner sandwich, and today there are döner shops in every neighbourhood.

Typical food in Berlin – Buletten: Buletten are Berlin-style meat patties, similar to small burgers without bread. They are usually eaten with mustard and are typical in traditional Berlin pubs (Kneipen).

Typical food in Berlin – Eisbein: Eisbein is boiled pork knuckle, a traditional Berlin dish, usually served with sauerkraut and pea purée (Erbspüree).

Typical drink in Berlin – Berliner Weisse: Berliner Weisse is a light, sour wheat beer from Berlin. It is often served with raspberry syrup (red) or woodruff syrup (green).

Typical sweet in Berlin – Berliner Pfannkuchen: The Berliner Pfannkuchen is a fried doughnut filled with jam and covered in sugar. In Berlin it is called "Pfannkuchen", while in other parts of Germany it is called "Berliner". It is especially popular on New Year's Eve and during Carnival.

Typical sweet in Berlin – Spritzkuchen: Spritzkuchen is a ring-shaped fried pastry with a sugar glaze, sold in many Berlin bakeries.

Food markets in Berlin: Markthalle Neun in Kreuzberg is a historic market hall with local food, and it hosts the popular Street Food Thursday.

Vegetarian and vegan food in Berlin: Berlin has one of the largest vegetarian and vegan food scenes in Europe. Most restaurants offer vegetarian options, and there are many fully vegan cafés and restaurants.
